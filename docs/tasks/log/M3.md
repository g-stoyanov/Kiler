# M3 log: the done tasks in full

The done tasks of [M3.md](../M3.md) with all their notes: the decisions made in the task, the measurements, the review passes and their fixes. A task moves here whole when it is ticked. - [x] **M3.1 Primitives, the encrypted value and the keys in memory**
  - What: PLAN M3 steps 1, 2 and 10. A key of 32 random bytes, held in a pinned array and zeroed when it is disposed; a key from a password with PBKDF2-SHA256, 600 000 iterations and a 16-byte salt (the password as UTF-8, as typed, its bytes zeroed after use); encrypting and decrypting a value in format 1 with its purpose and context as associated data (the decisions of the module). A value that is damaged, cut short, of another version or read with another purpose, context or key fails with one exception type, with no partial result and no secret in its message.
  - Tests (first): encrypting and decrypting gives the same bytes, an empty value too; a change of one byte in the version, the nonce, the ciphertext or the tag is detected, and so are another purpose, another context and another key; known test vectors for PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (RFC 7914, section 11) and AES-256-GCM with associated data (the test cases of the GCM specification), taken from the source by `docs-researcher`, not from memory; a value of format 1 written into the test as a constant decrypts, so a later format still reads it ("an old version of the format is read"); an unknown version, base64 that is not valid and a value shorter than 29 bytes fail; two encryptions of the same bytes differ; a disposed key is zeroed and cannot be used.
  - Done: the tests pass; `DependencyTests` passes; Kiler.Crypto has no package.
  - Note: the code. `SecretKey` (public: `New`, `Size`, `Dispose`; internal `FromBytes`, `Use`, `Buffer` for the tests), `PasswordKey` (`Iterations`, `SaltSize`, `MaxLength` 1024, `NewSalt`, `Derive`, and a `Derive` with any salt and count for the test vectors), `EncryptedValue` with `Purpose` (`Version1`, `MinSize` 29, `Encrypt`, `Decrypt`) and `Aes256Gcm` (`Create`, and `Encrypt`/`Decrypt` for the test vectors), all internal but `SecretKey`, reached by the tests through `InternalsVisibleTo`. Decided in the task: the text of a value must be the canonical base64 of its bytes, as `Convert` takes white space and bits set after the last byte; the parts of the context are encoded as strict UTF-8, as two lone surrogates would both become U+FFFD; a password over 1024 characters is an `ArgumentException`, so a huge one never makes a buffer of its size; a failed decryption is a `CryptographicException` with one message and no inner exception, and the plaintext is zeroed.
  - Note: the test vectors were taken from RFC 7914 (two copies) and the GCM specification of McGrew and Viega, Test Case 16, cross-checked with the test file of hostap, by `docs-researcher`. The value written earlier (`Decrypt_AValueOfFormat1WrittenEarlier_StillDecrypts`) was made once by this code with the key 0..31.
  - Note: the guards broken one at a time, each run with its test class: the canonical base64, the minimum size, the strict UTF-8 of the context, the zeroing on dispose, the disposed check and the lock of `Dispose` each made their test fail. The first try of the canonical base64 passed, as the values of `NotValues` were encrypted with another key than the test used, so every one of them failed at the tag; the test now uses their key and checks that the value they are made of decrypts. The version check is not proven alone (under "Left for after the exam").
  - Note: review, one pass of each (light cycle). Both found the same major: `Encrypt` took the span of the key at its start and gave it to `AesGcm` later, so a `Dispose` on another thread in between (the automatic locking of M4) zeroed the key, and the value was encrypted under zeros without an error. Fixed: the bytes are read only through `SecretKey.Use`, under the lock that `Dispose` takes, and `EncryptedValue` makes its `AesGcm` there (it copies the key); the test `Dispose_WaitsForAUseUnderWay` holds a use and sees `Dispose` wait. Of the minors, the docs were made exact (the 4-byte big-endian lengths in `kiler-crypto`, `crypto.md` in PLAN section 8 and CLAUDE.md, the name of the seal), the unpinned plaintext of a decrypted level key went to M3.3, the golden values of the other labels to M3.2 and M3.3, the password limit to M4, and the rest under "Left for after the exam" in M3.md.
- [x] **M3.2 Level keys, the check values and the field groups**
  - What: PLAN M3 steps 3 and 5. A new level key; the `check` value of a level (a known text encrypted with its key, purpose `check`) and its verification; encrypting and decrypting the group of fields of one level of a shared record (`protected."<level>"`, design 5.2), as the UTF-8 bytes that Kiler.Core gives, with purpose `group` and the record id and the level as context. Level 0 is not encrypted, so a level below 1 is refused.
  - Tests (first): a check verifies with the key of its level, and not with another key or for another level; a changed check fails; a group decrypts with its key, record id and level, and not under another record id, another level or another key; a changed byte of a group is detected; level 0 is refused.
  - Done: the tests pass.
  - Note: the code. Public `LevelCheck` (`Create`, `Verify`) and `FieldGroup` (`Encrypt`, `Decrypt`), and the internal `Level.Context`, which refuses a level below 1 and gives its digits. Decided in the task: a new level key stays `SecretKey.New()`; the known text of a check is the UTF-8 of `Kiler level check`, compared with `FixedTimeEquals` after the tag; `Verify` gives false for any `CryptographicException` (a damaged or foreign check, as `levels.json` is on the share) and lets `ObjectDisposedException` (a locked level) and `ArgumentException` (a bug of the caller) through; an empty record id is refused. The value written earlier for `check` (`Verify_ACheckWrittenEarlier_IsTrue`) was made once by this code with the key 0..31 and level 2; `FieldGroupTests` reads the group value of M3.1 through the public API, which pins the record id before the level.
  - Note: the 35 new tests were run first against stubs that threw `NotImplementedException`, and all failed. Then each guard was broken one at a time and its test class run: the known text not compared, the catch too wide or missing, another purpose for the check or the group, level 0 allowed, the level not as its digits, the context in the other order, the record id or the level left out of the context, an empty record id allowed. Each made its tests fail. The test `Decrypt_ACheck_Fails` was dropped, as a group has two parts of context and a check one, so it passed without the purposes.
  - Note: review, one pass of each (light cycle), with no critical or major finding. Code review: three minors, under "Left for after the exam" in M3.md. Security review: one minor, that inputs from the share (a null check or group, an empty record id or one with a lone surrogate) throw `ArgumentException` types, not false or `CryptographicException`; the code stays, and the rules for the callers went to "For later modules" for M4 and M5. Two `[minor, out of model]`, with no fix: a group does not carry the version of its record, so someone who can write the share can put back an older group of the same record and level (a deliberate edit, which design 7.8 marks); whoever can write `levels.json` can replace a check with one made with a key of their own, which decrypts nothing of the real key, as the seal is deferred (PLAN section 10). The second gave the M4 rule that a failed check never writes a new check or new level keys. After the commit the user accepted both: they are "The accepted limits" in the decisions of M3.md, and the admin seal is to cover `levels.json` too (PLAN section 10).
- [x] **M3.3 The user's keys and the recovery key**
  - What: PLAN M3 steps 4 and 7, without restoring admin access (PLAN section 10). Locking a set of level keys with a secret: a new salt, a key from PBKDF2, each level key encrypted with purpose `level-key` and its level as context (the user file of design 7.3: `salt` and `keys`); unlocking them; locking them again with a new password keeps the same level keys, with a new salt. The recovery key: a new one, its format, reading a typed key (the decisions of the module), and locking and unlocking the level keys with it.
  - Tests (first): a wrong password unlocks no key; a locked key moved to another level does not unlock; a new password keeps the same level keys; recovery with the recovery key, typed in lower case, with spaces and with O, I and L; a new recovery key has 8 groups of 4 characters of the alphabet, and two differ; a typed key with another character or another length is refused.
  - Done: the tests pass; Kiler.Crypto has full tests and no dependency on the other projects (PLAN M3, "Done").
  - From earlier tasks:
    - (M3.1) A level key decrypted from a `level-key` value must not stay in the unpinned `byte[]` that `EncryptedValue.Decrypt` gives: decrypt it into a pinned buffer that is zeroed, or straight into a `SecretKey`, for example with an internal `Decrypt` into a destination span (security review). The level key as the plaintext of a lock is read through `SecretKey.Use`.
    - (M3.1) `PasswordKey.Derive` throws `ArgumentException` for a salt that is not 16 bytes and a secret over `PasswordKey.MaxLength` (1024 characters). A salt comes from the user file on the share, so the unlock checks its length first and fails as a wrong password does.
    - (M3.1) Add a value written earlier for the label `level-key`, as `LevelCheckTests.Verify_ACheckWrittenEarlier_IsTrue` does for `check` (M3.2).
  - Note: the code. Public `LockedKeys` (`Salt`, `Keys` by level; null refused), `LevelKeys` (`Lock` and `Unlock`, each with a password or a `RecoveryKey`) and `RecoveryKey` (`New`, `TryParse`, `ToDisplayString`, `Dispose`; internal `FromBytes`, `Secret`, `Buffer`), and an internal `EncryptedValue.Decrypt` into a destination span, which refuses a value of another length. Decided in the task: `Unlock` gives only the keys that unlock, as the set comes from the share and design 7.5 checks each key against its check and refuses the sign-in only when none is valid; a wrong password, or a salt that is not 16 bytes of base64, gives an empty set, and a damaged value, a key moved to another level, a value that does not hold 32 bytes and an entry of a level below 1 or with a null value are left out. Each key that unlocks is decrypted into one pinned buffer, zeroed in a `finally`. `Lock` refuses a level below 1 and a null key before PBKDF2, and a disposed key with `ObjectDisposedException`. The recovery key keeps its 32 characters in a pinned `char` array, zeroed on dispose, with no lock, as it serves one request; `ToString` is the type's name; upper case is ASCII only, not by the culture; only `-` and U+0020 are skipped. `New` takes 20 random bytes and writes each 5 bits as a character, from the highest bit of the first byte on; the test vector is the 20 bytes whose groups are 0 to 31, whose RFC 4648 base32 is that alphabet in order (checked with Python's `base64.b32encode`), so their Crockford Base32 is the alphabet in order. The value written earlier for `level-key` was made by this code: password `demo password`, the keys 0 to 31 and 32 to 63 for levels 1 and 2.
  - Note: the 33 new test methods (64 cases) were run first against stubs that threw `NotImplementedException`, and all failed. Then 17 guards were broken one at a time and their test class run. Two did not fail at first. The salt length: a salt of 15 bytes went into the 16-byte buffer with a zero after it, and the values of the test were not locked with that salt, so they failed at the tag anyway; the new test `Unlock_ASaltOneByteShort_GivesNoKey` locks with a salt that ends with a zero byte and gives it without that byte. The ASCII upper case, broken with `char.ToUpperInvariant`: .NET's invariant casing maps no letter outside ASCII into it (not the dotless i, not the Kelvin sign), so the break changed nothing; broken with the upper case of the culture instead, it fails the new test `TryParse_UnderATurkishCulture_ReadsASmallI` and the dotless i of `NotKeys`. The other 15 failed their tests: the level below 1 and the null value in `Unlock`, another purpose, the context without the level, the length of the destination, the catch, O as 0, L as 1, too many characters, the zeroing and the disposed check of the recovery key, the bit order, a new salt per lock, the display form as the secret, the spaces. Checks: `format.ps1` and the tests of Kiler.Crypto, 143 passing.
  - Note: review, one pass of each (light cycle), with no critical or major finding. Both found that the summary of the `Decrypt` into a span promises a zeroed destination for every failure, and that `RecoveryKey` has no lock against a `Dispose` during `Lock`. The security review found that `Lock` takes an empty password, that `RecoveryKey` lacks the finalizer and the checked `Buffer` as `SecretKey` does (the M3.1 minors), and that a change of password that locks again only what unlocked would drop a damaged level. The code review found the cast before the mask in the Base32, the `catch` with no reachable cause, and the wording of the JSON keys. All minor: under "Left for after the exam" in M3.md, and the rules for M4 (the partial unlock and the change of password, the minimum password length, the lifetime of a recovery key, the old password in `history\`) under "For later modules".
